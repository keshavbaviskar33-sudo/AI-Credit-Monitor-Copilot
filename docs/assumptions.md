> **What is still open, and why each one is.** Six assumptions remain open and
> none of them is open through neglect.
>
> - **[A-01](#users--workflow), [A-02](#users--workflow)** — the analyst
>   workflow and the claim that analysts value change and contradictions over a
>   score. **Not closeable by this project:** there is no access to practising
>   analysts, and a scripted walkthrough by the author is not evidence about
>   users ([R-20](risks.md)). A-02 is load-bearing — the entire
>   no-composite-score design rests on it — and that is stated rather than
>   hidden.
> - **[A-03](#users--workflow)** — whether an annual cadence is enough. Open
>   after the Phase 11 revisit: the period-over-period machinery exists and is
>   unit-tested, but no multi-period replay was run, so nothing measures what
>   an annual cadence misses.
> - **[A-06](#data)** — concept mapping. Validated on 12 filings and explicitly
>   not beyond them.
> - **[A-16](#nlp--llm)** — partially validated at n=12, on one provider. The
>   one unpaid measurement debt in the project.
> - **[A-18](#technical--legal)** — the monolith-plus-SQLite premise. Never
>   stressed: 202 assessments, 4.46 MB, 25 ms queries. Revisit only on a
>   concrete requirement, as written.
> - **[A-20](#technical--legal)** — the legal basis for SEC data. High
>   confidence, obligations met (declared User-Agent, rate limiting,
>   attribution), but it is a legal reading rather than a measurement, so it is
>   not something an engineering phase can mark validated.

Every assumption here is something the design depends on but has **not yet been
proven**. Each has a validation plan. When one is validated or disproven, update
its status and, if it changes the design, add a decision-log entry.

**Confidence:** High = well-documented or verified at source · Medium = plausible, partly supported · Low = uncertain; could change the design.
**Status:** Open · Validated · Invalidated.

## Data

| ID | Assumption | Why it matters | Confidence | Validation (how / when) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-04 | SEC XBRL APIs are free, need no API key, and allow ≤ 10 requests/second with a declared User-Agent | Core data source for MVP | High — verified against SEC developer documentation, 2026-09-13 | Confirm in Phase 3 with a real request | **Validated (closed in Phase 19 against evidence from Phase 3 onward).** The plan was executed and then some: the Phase 3 linkage audit ran **992 live `companyfacts` requests** with no key ([D-013](decision_log.md)), and every corpus build since has run against the live API. `sec_edgar.py` throttles at 8 req/s against the 10 req/s limit and *refuses to run* without a configured User-Agent. **Never rate-limited or blocked** across every phase ([R-14](risks.md)) |
| A-05 | XBRL `companyfacts` provides, per fact, the accession number, form type and filing date needed for point-in-time selection | Point-in-time correctness (SC-07) depends on it | High → **confirmed** | Inspect a live response in Phase 3 | **Validated (closed in Phase 19).** Per-fact `accn`/`form`/`fy`/`fp`/`filed` confirmed present in Phase 3 ([D-013](decision_log.md)), and the whole point-in-time contract was then *built on it and tested*: three redundant enforcement layers ([D-026](decision_log.md)), and **202 of 202** assessments refused when dated one day early ([D-039](decision_log.md)). Had the metadata been absent or unreliable, none of that would run |
| A-06 | Core line items (revenue, current assets/liabilities, total assets, debt, equity, operating cash flow, interest expense) can be mapped from US-GAAP XBRL concepts for most in-scope companies | Ratio coverage | **Medium→Low-Medium (Phase 4)→Medium-High (Phase 5 audit)** | Per-concept coverage over the golden set ([data_dictionary.md §6.1](data_dictionary.md)); QM-01 after tag policies and derivation ([canonical_schema.md §12](canonical_schema.md)) | **Validated for the golden set, not with a single-tag lookup.** Revenue, net income, operating cash flow, total assets and total liabilities resolve for every presented period; `total_debt` 86%, `interest_expense` 92%. Validated on 12 filings only — confirm on a larger sample before relying on it for the full universe |
| A-07 | Text-based PDFs of 10-K financial statements can be obtained or generated for a golden evaluation set | Needed to measure extraction accuracy against XBRL | **High — confirmed Phase 4** | Golden set assembled by rendering filing HTML to PDF ([D-014](decision_log.md), [golden_set.md](golden_set.md)) | **Confirmed, with a caveat:** generated PDFs are cleaner than real-world uploads, so QM-01's PDF figure is an upper bound |
| A-08 | A US corporate dataset with a distress label exists that is usable for a public portfolio project | The ML layer depends on it | Medium → **High** | Phase 3 decision gate ([data_feasibility.md](data_feasibility.md)) | **Validated (closed in Phase 19).** The gate resolved in [D-013](decision_log.md): the Florida-UCLA-LoPucki BRD, licensed for commercial and academic use with attribution, joined to SEC XBRL by CIK — **178 cases with ≥2 pre-bankruptcy annual periods**, above the pre-registered "100+" bar. Two caveats the assumption did not anticipate and the project now carries everywhere: BRD records only *large public* bankruptcies, which is the cohort confound [D-054](decision_log.md) had to match its way out of, and it stopped updating in December 2022 |
| A-09 | Features available in the training data can be reproduced from SEC XBRL data with equivalent definitions | Avoids training/serving skew | **Low → not applicable (Phase 17)** | Feature-by-feature mapping in Phase 3; quantify mismatch | **Superseded by [D-013](decision_log.md), with the residual measured.** The assumption presumed *two* feature definitions — a third-party training set's and SEC XBRL's. The self-built corpus left one: `modeling/features.py` runs at both training and inference, so there is no second definition to skew against. The residual is temporal, not definitional — the same period resolved from a later filing can differ, measured by QM-04 at **453 restatements over 153 filings, median 0.8% but p90 20.0% and 23.6% above 5%** ([measurement_debts.md §6](measurement_debts.md)). [D-008](decision_log.md)/[D-026](decision_log.md) answer it by construction; that table is the size of what they avoid. Propagation into ratio and feature values is still unmeasured |
| A-10 | MD&A and Risk Factors sections can be located in 10-K HTML reliably enough for NLP | NLP input quality | **Partially confirmed (Phase 10)** — measured at scale, and lower than the golden set implied | Measured over 238 filings ([nlp_risk_signals.md §8](nlp_risk_signals.md)): Item 7 **83.6%**, Item 1A **81.5%**, both **81.1%**. The Phase 4 golden set's 12/12 did not generalise | **Resolved as a measured ceiling, not a defect.** Roughly one filing in five yields no MD&A, so "no signals" means "not read" for that fifth — `SectionCoverage` records which, so the two are distinguishable downstream. Improving heading detection is Phase 4 work, not Phase 10's |

## ML

| ID | Assumption | Why it matters | Confidence | Validation (how / when) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-11 | Bankruptcy filing (or the best available label) is a reasonable proxy for "credit distress" for a monitoring aid | Defines what the model estimate means | Medium — bankruptcy is rare and late; earlier deterioration is not labelled | Document label semantics in Phase 3; state limits in model card | **Accepted as a proxy, with the miss measured (Phase 17).** The failure is one-directional: a company can disclose severe distress and never file, and that filing is labelled 0. Over 238 filings, counting only *asserted* disclosures: `going_concern_doubt` is followed by a petition **27 of 32** times, and **any** distress disclosure only **59 of 92** (64.1%) ([measurement_debts.md §6](measurement_debts.md)). So bankruptcy-within-365-days is a **strict subset** of credit distress, and 33 genuinely distressed filings train the model to call them safe. The corpus is year-matched at base rate 0.496, so the *direction* is the finding and the level is not portfolio-transferable |
| A-12 | Logistic regression on financial ratios gives a useful, explainable baseline | Justifies starting simple | High | Compare to transparent ratio baseline, Phase 8–9 | **Validated, and it outlived its role as a baseline (closed in Phase 19).** Useful: it beats every heuristic comparator, including Phase 7's own deterioration count used as a score (0.803 vs 0.716 pooled). Explainable: its contributions are **exact in closed form**, reconstructing the model's own output to 3.6e-15, which is why [D-030](decision_log.md) measured SHAP as unnecessary for it. Two things the assumption did not foresee. It was **demoted** — [D-034](decision_log.md) made gradient boosting primary, because on the confound-free population three of logistic's top seven drivers are *missingness indicators*. And it is nonetheless the model carrying the project's **one quotable bankruptcy figure**, 0.817 [0.778, 0.851], because it is the one whose confound closes ([D-054](decision_log.md)). Useful and explainable, then — but the simpler model was not the more trustworthy one on either count the assumption implied |
| A-13 | Enough positive events exist for a meaningful **out-of-time** test | Otherwise metrics are too noisy to trust | Medium | Count events per year in Phase 3; report confidence intervals | **Validated pooled, invalidated per fold (Phase 17).** 183 events over 7 walk-forward folds, **9 to 43 per fold** — a nine-event fold cannot support a per-fold metric, and none is reported. [D-027](decision_log.md) pools the out-of-fold scores instead, and the company-clustered intervals that result are **0.056–0.090 wide** ([measurement_debts.md §6](measurement_debts.md)). That width is the honest expression of the event count: differences below ~0.05 are not readable from two intervals, which is exactly why [D-034](decision_log.md) used a *paired* test |
| A-14 | The training base rate differs from any real portfolio's, so raw probabilities need careful framing | Prevents misleading "74% risk" displays | High | Calibration analysis and explicit labelling, Phase 9 | **Validated, decisively (Phase 17).** The real-world rate is **0.39%** — 367 BRD petitions over 93,394 SEC annual-filer-years, both sides restricted to BRD's own "public" condition — against design base rates of **0.049** (point-in-time), **0.081** (eligibility-matched) and **0.496** (the year-matched narrative and assessment corpora). The training base rate is **12× to 126× the real one** ([measurement_debts.md §6](measurement_debts.md)). The framing the assumption asked for is already enforced in the type by [D-033](decision_log.md), not in prose |

## NLP & LLM

| ID | Assumption | Why it matters | Confidence | Validation (how / when) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-15 | Rules plus classical NLP give acceptable precision for key signals before any LLM extraction is needed | Keeps NLP cheap, deterministic and testable | **Confirmed for the signals that matter (Phase 10)** | **73.4% precision** overall on 64 hand-labelled signals, but **13/13** on the three highest-lift codes (going concern, bankruptcy contemplated, delisting notice) ([nlp_risk_signals.md §9](nlp_risk_signals.md)). No LLM, no model, no training data | **Accepted; recall now measured, with a named blind spot (Phase 17).** Precision is concentrated where it matters: the statutory disclosures are exact, the discretionary prose is where the misses are. Every false positive traces to one `pattern_id`, which is the property that made six classes of them fixable in an afternoon ([D-035](decision_log.md)). Recall is **38.1% [20.8%, 59.1%] relative to the sweep's reach** ([D-055](decision_log.md)), and the pool independently re-confirms precision at 8/8. But 16 of its 21 true instances are `asset_impairment`, and **six of twelve codes have no true instance in it at all** — including two of the three highest-lift codes — so the figure characterises impairment detection more than the catalog |
| A-16 | A single structured LLM call can produce a grounded synthesis when given evidence with IDs | Keeps the synthesis layer simple | **Partially validated (Phase 12)** | Two measurements. The validator the validation plan called for is complete and measured by fault injection — 0.000 false-positive rate, 100% detection across seven fault classes on 176 assessments ([grounded_synthesis.md §5](grounded_synthesis.md)). A live run then produced **12 drafts on `gemini-3.6-flash`, 11 accepted (91.7%)** ([§8](grounded_synthesis.md), [D-044](decision_log.md)) | **Confirmed once, at small n, and the failure mode is real.** One structured call over an evidence pack does produce a grounded, citable synthesis. But 12 drafts put a 95% interval of roughly 62%–100% on that rate, so it is not a reliability figure — and the single rejection was a model silently dropping a word from inside a quotation it was told to copy verbatim, which is exactly why the assumption was never allowed to be load-bearing on its own. **Phase 17 was meant to take this past n=12 on a second provider and could not: `LLM_PROVIDER` and `LLM_API_KEY` are empty and the Phase 12 credential is gone ([measurement_debts.md §5](measurement_debts.md)). The Anthropic client has still never made a call.** This is the one debt of five that remains unpaid, and it is reported as unpaid rather than substituted |
| A-17 | Management language is often optimistic, so optimistic tone is weak evidence against deteriorating numbers | Contradiction rules must not treat tone as equal to financial data | **Confirmed in a stronger form than stated (Phase 11)** | The design implication is met structurally: no layer in this project produces a tone read, so no contradiction rule can weigh one — the narrative layer emits *disclosure statements* with an assertion mood, not sentiment ([D-035](decision_log.md)). What Phase 11 adds is the number the assumption was reaching for. At a matched alert budget the narrative layer costs **20 precision points** against the model (−0.200 [−0.307, −0.082]), and `HEALTH_AND_NARRATIVE` agreement costs 19.8 ([combined_assessment.md §6.2](combined_assessment.md)); the filer's own words, in either direction, are the weaker evidence | **Accepted.** The rules never treat the filer's language as equal to the financial layer, and the measurement says they should not. One exception is measured and kept: a filing that both asserts *and* denies the same condition is 2.04× more likely to precede a petition — the contradiction *between* two statements is informative where either alone is not |
